Mid-Level Java Backend Interview Roadmap

An 18-week plan for working through the topic map: October 26, 2026 to February 28, 2027, with extra buffer time through March 14. The phases follow what comes up most often in real interviews and what you need to know before moving on. Databases and Spring run in parallel and wrap up before the holidays, since the first round of interviews leans heavily on both. Daily tracks and checkpoints run alongside the phases. Linked topics open their pages on the site.

Starts Monday, October 26, 2026 About 20 hours per week: 2.5 hours per weekday, 4 hours per weekend day Start interviewing around week 3 or 4 while you keep studying Holiday break, Dec. 28–Jan. 10: 50–60% workload, for deep dives and project work School: winter exams (dates to be confirmed); JavaScript course project due Dec. 25, 2026 Buffer: Feb. 15–28; if you pass fewer than a third of the topics on the diagnostic, take two more weeks through March 14 Adjust the schedule based on mini-exams and mock interviews

Test what you know

Don't skip or mark a topic complete based on how well you think you know it. Take a diagnostic, answer from memory, and get scored against a rubric. Rereading makes you feel confident without making it stick: after a week, people who reread remembered 40% of the text; those who tested themselves remembered 61%.

Two passes

First, cover the essentials across all subject areas so you can start interviewing early. Then work through topics in dependency order: equals → HashMap → ConcurrentHashMap, JMM → j.u.c, proxies → AOP → @Transactional.

Level 3 is mid-level

Explain why it works that way, what the trade-offs are, and where people trip up. For the most common questions, aim for level 4: show code, diagnose problems, give an example from your own project, and handle two or three follow-ups.

Three sessions to mark a topic complete

Answer correctly on day 0, again on day 3 or later, and once more on day 10 or later. Mix the topic in with others at least once. Get Claude to score you against the rubric, ask a practice partner, or use a real interview. Don't grade yourself.

Demos over notes

For the most common questions, build a reproducible test in your lab project. In an interview, you want to say, “I reproduced it, and here are the numbers,” instead of reciting an article.

Interviews are part of prep

Start around week 3 or 4 with companies that aren't your top picks. After each interview, add the questions to your question bank and review anything you missed over the next three days.

Tiers A and B

A: go to level 3. Know how it works, why it works that way, and what the trade-offs are. Spend 25–45 minutes per topic. B: know what it is and when to use it (level 1–2). Spend 10–15 minutes per topic; these are marked “B.” Anything you pass on the diagnostic goes into B too.

What employers expect in 2026

The sample compares 107 mid-level Java backend job listings from hh.ru, Habr Career, and getmatch (September 15–October 22, 2026) with 64 mid-level listings from Habr Career in late 2021, using the same skill vocabulary for both.

The idea that “mid-level roles ask for a lot more” held up, but the range of skills didn't grow much. The median number of skills per listing only went from 14.5 to 16, and mid-level roles already asked for microservices, Docker, and Kubernetes in 2021. What grew was depth and competition: database performance (indexes and query plans) went from 6% to 30% of listings, fault tolerance (retries, circuit breakers, and rate limiting) from 2% to 21%, Kafka from 41% to 65%, PostgreSQL from 59% to 82%, Prometheus and Grafana from 5% to 15%, and Testcontainers from 2% to 10%. Java 17/21 replaced Java 8/11. There are 14.2 résumés per IT job opening, up from 4.4 in 2021.

A hands-on round is now a must: code review, a SQL problem, or live coding. A lighter system design round shows up in roughly a third of mid-level interviews; in 2021 it was mostly for senior roles. Agile (39% → 11%), Oracle (28% → 14%), and English (16% → 6%) appear in fewer job listings.

Phase 0 · week 1

Diagnostic and setup

Get an honest baseline across the important subject areas and set up what you'll use for the next 16 weeks: review sessions, a mistake log, a lab, a résumé, and job applications.

Why now: The diagnostic helps the material stick later and shows where “I know this” doesn't hold up. Start applying in week 1: it usually takes a week or two to get from application to interview.

Tier A diagnostic

Take 10–15 questions from each area and answer them out loud without prep, rating your confidence from 1 to 3. Claude scores them from 0 to 4. Any topics in “OOP,” “Strings,” or “Collections” that you don't pass automatically move to phase 2: outsourcing firms can run a rapid-fire round of about 30 topics in 90 minutes.

Java under the hood: the language, JVM, and multithreading

Finish Tier A for Java: the language, memory and garbage collection, and multithreading from the JMM through virtual threads. Save the JVM deep dive for the holidays.

Why now: This is where the longest dependency chains are: memory areas → GC → leaks → OOMKilled; JMM → volatile and synchronized → j.u.c → CompletableFuture and virtual threads. The college materials didn't cover garbage collection at all. Java 17/21 is explicitly listed in 27% of job openings, and multithreading in 36%. These are core topics in the Java rounds at T-Bank and Yandex.

Spaced repetition 20–30 minutes every day

Use Anki with FSRS and a target retention of 0.90. Add no more than 10–15 new cards a day. Each card should have one fact or a “why” question with a 3–5-point checklist on the back. Only press “Good” if you hit every point.
